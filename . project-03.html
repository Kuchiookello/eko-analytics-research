Project Overview

From routine programme data to actionable management insight

Youth empowerment programmes often collect substantial amounts of operational information through enrolment forms, attendance registers, training records, baseline assessments, endline assessments and post-programme follow-up.

The challenge is turning those records into a coherent monitoring system that helps programme managers understand whether activities are being delivered as planned, whether participants remain engaged, whether skills are improving and whether programme participation is translating into meaningful employment-related outcomes.

Project 03 demonstrates a structured M&E approach using a synthetic dataset of 400 beneficiaries across Nairobi, Kiambu, Nakuru, Kisumu and Mombasa.

Project Scope

  • 400 synthetic beneficiary records
  • 5 counties
  • 4 training tracks
  • January–August 2026 programme period
  • 8 core performance indicators
  • County and training-track comparisons
  • Management dashboard and analytical report

Portfolio Transparency

This is an independent EKO Analytics & Research portfolio case study. All data are synthetic and were created for demonstration purposes. The findings do not represent a real youth programme, implementing organisation, county or labour market.

Objectives

What the analysis was designed to answer

Programme Reach

Track how many beneficiaries were enrolled and how participation was distributed across counties, gender groups and training tracks.

Participation & Retention

Assess completion, attendance and dropout to understand whether participants remained consistently engaged.

Skills Development

Compare baseline and endline assessment scores to measure changes in participant capability.

Employment Outcomes

Measure the proportion of participants moving into employment, self-employment or internships.

Programme Delivery

Compare planned and completed activities to assess implementation performance.

Management Learning

Translate programme evidence into practical recommendations for stronger participation, delivery and post-training outcomes.

Methodology

How the monitoring framework was structured

1. Define Indicators

Establish measures for reach, completion, attendance, skills change, employment outcomes, gender participation, activity completion and dropout.

2. Structure Data

Organise beneficiary and programme-delivery records into clean, analysis-ready datasets.

3. Calculate Performance

Compute actual indicator values and compare them with programme targets.

4. Disaggregate Results

Compare performance by county and training track to identify meaningful differences.

5. Visualise Evidence

Present key indicators through a management dashboard for faster interpretation.

6. Translate to Action

Use the evidence to identify programme priorities, operational gaps and learning opportunities.

Key Findings

What the evidence shows

Reach Target Achieved

The programme enrolled all 400 planned beneficiaries, demonstrating full achievement of the reach target.

Completion Slightly Below Target

The 72.3% completion rate fell slightly below the 75% target, suggesting a need for stronger retention measures.

Attendance Is the Main Operational Gap

Average attendance was 67.1% against an 80% target, making participation consistency the clearest management priority.

Skills Outcomes Are Encouraging

Average skills improvement reached 20.4 points, exceeding the 18-point programme target.

Employment Outcomes Exceeded Target

56.8% of beneficiaries recorded employment, self-employment or internship outcomes, compared with a 50% target.

Implementation Delivery Was Strong

97.3% of planned programme sessions were completed, indicating strong implementation capacity.

Management Interpretation

What programme managers should pay attention to

Delivery is not the same as engagement

The programme delivered 97.3% of planned activities, yet average beneficiary attendance was only 67.1%. Strong implementation volume therefore did not automatically translate into consistent participant engagement.

Outcomes remain promising

Skills improvement and employment-related outcomes exceeded their respective targets. This suggests that the programme model can generate value, particularly for beneficiaries who remain actively engaged.

Recommendations

Priority actions for programme improvement

Strengthen Attendance Monitoring

Review attendance weekly and trigger early follow-up where beneficiaries begin missing sessions.

Investigate Participation Barriers

Use short interviews or pulse surveys to understand whether timing, transport, work commitments or programme relevance affect attendance.

Target Post-Training Support

Strengthen employer, internship and self-employment linkages in locations where employment outcomes remain weaker.

Learn From Stronger Locations

Document practices associated with stronger outcomes and test whether they can be adapted elsewhere.

Add Follow-Up Measurement

Track employment and livelihood outcomes at three and six months after programme completion.

Use the Dashboard for Decisions

Integrate indicator review into routine programme-management meetings and record actions taken in response to performance gaps.

Limitations

Responsible interpretation

The dataset is synthetic and designed solely to demonstrate EKO Analytics & Research's analytical and M&E capability. A live assignment would require validated source records, agreed indicator definitions, beneficiary consent and data-protection procedures, programme-specific targets, contextual interpretation and appropriate outcome-verification methods.
