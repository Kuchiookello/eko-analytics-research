PROJECT 03 • MONITORING & EVALUATION

Youth Empowerment Programme M&E Dashboard

A monitoring and evaluation case study examining programme reach, participation, completion, attendance, skills development, employment outcomes and programme-delivery performance.

CASE STUDY OVERVIEW

Turning programme data into evidence for management and learning.

Project 03 demonstrates how monitoring and evaluation data can be transformed into practical evidence for programme management, accountability and learning.

The case study examines a synthetic youth empowerment programme involving 400 beneficiaries across Nairobi, Kiambu, Nakuru, Kisumu and Mombasa.

Beneficiary participation, programme implementation, skills development and employment-related outcomes are analysed through a structured indicator framework.


ANALYTICAL QUESTIONS

What the project investigates

  • How many beneficiaries were reached by the programme?
  • What proportion successfully completed their training?
  • How consistently did beneficiaries participate in programme activities?
  • To what extent did participant skills improve?
  • What employment, self-employment or internship outcomes were achieved?
  • How did performance differ across counties and training tracks?
  • Which programme indicators require management attention?

KEY FINDINGS

What the programme evidence shows.

Programme Reach

The programme enrolled 400 beneficiaries, achieving the planned beneficiary-reach target.

Completion

The completion rate reached 72.3%, slightly below the programme target of 75%.

Attendance Gap

Average attendance was 67.1% against an 80% target, making participation consistency a key management concern.

Skills Improvement

Average skills improvement reached 20.4 points, exceeding the programme target of 18 points.

Employment Outcomes

Employment-related outcomes reached 56.8%, exceeding the programme target of 50%.

Programme Delivery

97.3% of planned activities were completed, indicating strong implementation performance.

Management Interpretation

Mombasa recorded the strongest employment outcome rate at 66.7%, while Kisumu recorded the lowest at 44.3%. Completion rates were comparatively similar across counties, suggesting that differences in post-training opportunities may be contributing more strongly to employment variation than completion alone.

MANAGEMENT INTERPRETATION

Strong programme delivery, but weaker beneficiary attendance.

WHAT IS WORKING

Delivery and outcomes are encouraging.

Programme implementation was strong, with 97.3% of planned activities completed.

Skills improvement exceeded target, while employment-related outcomes reached 56.8%, also above target.

Female participation reached 51%, meeting the programme's participation objective.

MANAGEMENT PRIORITY

Attendance requires immediate attention.

Despite strong programme delivery, average attendance was only 67.1% against an 80% target.

This suggests that delivering planned activities alone does not guarantee consistent beneficiary engagement.

Attendance should therefore become a priority indicator in routine programme-management discussions.

RECOMMENDATIONS

Priority actions for programme improvement.

Attendance Early Warning

Introduce routine follow-up for beneficiaries whose attendance begins to decline.

Participant Feedback

Use short beneficiary pulse interviews to understand barriers affecting participation and retention.

County-Level Follow-Up

Investigate weaker employment outcomes in locations where post-training results fall below programme averages.

Employment Linkages

Strengthen employer, internship and self-employment partnerships for programme graduates.

Routine Dashboard Review

Integrate the dashboard into regular management meetings to identify performance gaps early.

Outcome Follow-Up

Track employment and livelihood outcomes three and six months after programme completion.

PORTFOLIO TRANSPARENCY

Demonstration data used responsibly.

Project 03 is an independent EKO Analytics & Research portfolio case study.

The programme and beneficiary data used in this project are synthetic and were created solely to demonstrate monitoring, evaluation, analysis, dashboard and management-reporting capability.

The findings should therefore not be interpreted as evidence about an actual youth programme, organisation, county programme or labour market.
