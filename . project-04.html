PROJECT 04 • MARKET RESEARCH

Consumer Purchasing Behaviour & Market Opportunity Study

A market-research case study examining consumer behaviour, spending patterns, purchasing channels, switching behaviour, unmet needs and relative market opportunities.

CASE STUDY OVERVIEW

Understanding customers before making market decisions.

Project 04 demonstrates how EKO Analytics & Research can transform consumer survey data into practical market intelligence for organisations considering new products, services, customer segments or market opportunities.

The study analyses a synthetic dataset of 500 consumers across five Kenyan urban markets, examining purchasing behaviour, spending, satisfaction, channel preferences, willingness to switch brands, purchase intent and unmet needs.

Rather than relying on a single indicator, the analysis combines several consumer signals to identify areas that may justify deeper market validation and concept testing.


RESEARCH QUESTIONS

What the study investigates

  • What factors influence consumer purchasing decisions?
  • How much are consumers spending across different product categories?
  • Which purchasing channels do consumers prefer?
  • How willing are consumers to switch from their current brands?
  • What unmet needs influence purchasing behaviour?
  • Which categories show stronger relative market-opportunity signals?

KEY FINDINGS

What the consumer evidence suggests.

Price strongly influences purchasing behaviour.

Price emerged as the leading purchase driver, while lower prices were also the most frequently identified unmet need. Affordability therefore represents an important proposition-design issue.

Consumers show meaningful willingness to switch.

45.6% of respondents indicated willingness to switch brands, suggesting that stronger value propositions may be able to attract customers from existing alternatives.

Digital channels matter, but do not dominate.

Online Marketplace, Brand Website and Social Commerce together represent 38.2% of preferred purchasing channels. The result supports an omnichannel rather than digital-only approach.

Electronics shows the strongest relative signal.

Electronics recorded the highest relative opportunity score in the model, supported by an average monthly spend of approximately KES 11,711 and the highest purchase-intent score of 3.80/5.

Demand volume and opportunity are not identical.

Food & Groceries recorded the largest demand share at 25.4%, demonstrating why category size should be considered alongside spending, switching and purchase-intent indicators.

Market signals require deeper validation.

The analysis identifies areas for further investigation rather than automatically recommending market entry. Concept testing, pricing research and competitor analysis would be required before commercial decisions.

RELATIVE OPPORTUNITY

Electronics emerges as a category for deeper concept testing.

Electronics represented 11.2% of category demand within the synthetic dataset, but recorded the highest average monthly spend at approximately KES 11,711 and the highest average purchase intent at 3.80/5.

When demand, spending, willingness to switch, purchase intent and satisfaction gap were combined, Electronics produced the strongest relative opportunity score.

This does not establish Electronics as the objectively best market opportunity. It identifies the category as a strong candidate for deeper validation through concept testing, pricing analysis and competitor research.

OPPORTUNITY MODEL

Multiple signals were considered.

  • Demand share — 25%
  • Average spending — 15%
  • Willingness to switch — 20%
  • Purchase intent — 25%
  • Satisfaction gap — 15%

The resulting score is a relative analytical tool for prioritising further research. It is not a market-size estimate or sales forecast.

MANAGEMENT INTERPRETATION

What could an organisation do with these findings?

The analysis suggests that an organisation considering market expansion should avoid basing its decision on demand volume alone.

  • Test the Electronics proposition more deeply before committing resources to market entry.
  • Examine willingness to pay rather than assuming that lower prices automatically create the strongest commercial proposition.
  • Develop an omnichannel customer journey combining digital access with reliable physical fulfilment and customer support.
  • Segment the analysis further by age, income, location and channel preference.
  • Benchmark competitor pricing, availability, service experience and positioning.
  • Combine consumer demand evidence with commercial measures such as market size, margin, acquisition cost and retention.
