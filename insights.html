DECISION-MAKING

Data is useful only when it changes the decision.

A dashboard can be visually impressive and still fail to answer the question management actually faces.

Good analytics begins with the decision. Before calculating indicators or designing charts, an organisation should ask: what are we trying to understand, what decision will this evidence inform, and what would we do differently if the result changes?

This shifts analytics away from producing information for its own sake and toward producing evidence that is directly connected to action.

RESEARCH

Good research begins before data collection.

Strong research depends on clear objectives, well-defined questions and an appropriate method.

If the research question is unclear, collecting more data does not necessarily improve the result. In fact, it can create more information without creating more understanding.

Careful research design ensures that every interview, questionnaire, dataset or document review contributes to answering the actual question.

RESEARCH CHECK
What is the research objective?
What questions must be answered?
What evidence is actually needed?
How will the findings be used?

SME ANALYTICS

Small businesses do not need “big data” to make better decisions.

Many SMEs assume analytics requires large databases, expensive software or specialist systems.

In practice, basic transaction records, customer information, product data and monthly sales can already reveal useful patterns when the information is cleaned, structured and analysed consistently.

A simple monthly dashboard can help management see revenue trends, high-value customers, strong-performing services and emerging performance gaps.

REPORTING

The best report is not necessarily the longest report.

Long documents can create an impression of depth, but length does not guarantee usefulness.

Decision-makers generally need four things: what was found, what evidence supports it, why it matters and what should happen next.

Analytical depth should strengthen clarity rather than bury the reader under unnecessary detail.

EKO PERSPECTIVE

Evidence should simplify the decision, not complicate it.

Organisations increasingly have access to more information, but more information does not automatically mean greater clarity.

The role of research and analytics is therefore not simply to produce additional data. It is to organise uncertainty, test assumptions, identify relevant patterns and make the decision environment clearer.

EKO ANALYTICAL PRINCIPLE

Clarity is an analytical outcome.

Good analysis should leave the decision-maker with a stronger understanding of what matters.
